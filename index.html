AI Foundations Course

Overfit & Chill

Twelve classes. No formulas on the first day. The math arrives when you need it.

This course is for people who work with data, commission data products, or manage teams that build them. You will not leave knowing how to train the next language model. You will leave knowing what questions to ask when someone shows you one.

Every topic has a direct track and an optional deep dive for the curious. The tags tell you how long something takes and how much code is involved.

05 Regularization When the model fits too well. ›
A model that memorizes the training data is not learning — it is cheating. Regularization is the art of teaching a model to forget just enough to generalize.

Ridge, Lasso, and the Art of Shrinkage
A hands-on Colab notebook. Start with a model that overfits badly, then add L2 (Ridge) and L1 (Lasso) penalties and watch the coefficients shrink. Lasso goes further — it zeros some features out entirely, which is feature selection in disguise. Tune the regularization strength and observe the bias-variance trade-off in action.

Modelling Decisions
From Verhulst's rabbits to a vote in Bundestag. The sigmoid turns a sum of scores into a probability between zero and one. Three games — tilt the decision boundary, watch each feature deposit its log-odds, and drag the threshold to see precision trade places with recall.

Pilzwanderung — The Four Mistakes
Precision, recall, accuracy, F1. Four numbers arguing about the same basket of mushrooms. Pick a threshold, watch dinner turn into a funeral. One demonstration of why ninety-nine percent accuracy can still kill you.
mini-module ~40 min no-code try-without-ai bayes inference
Thinking in Chances
A patient tests positive on a ninety-nine percent accurate test. Most people say ninety-nine. The answer is closer to fifty. Three games — a mammogram, an urn, and Turing's Banburismus at Bletchley Park — all built on the same arithmetic of changing your mind.
